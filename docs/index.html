36 keys · PG1316S · XIAO nRF52840 · ZMK

An ultra-thin wireless split, two clean rectangles

A pocketable 36-key keyboard with no row or column stagger. It uses Kailh PG1316S laptop-style butterfly switches, a 1 mm PCB and a 3D-printed tray. The target is 7 mm or less from desk to keycap.

Build phases

00Requirements & decisions

  • Thin and portable. 7 mm or less, including keycaps and case. The closest prior build, vuoz/chalk, reaches about 6 mm.
  • Pure ortholinear. No row stagger and no column stagger. Each half is a clean rectangle.
  • Wireless split with long standby and a rechargeable battery.
  • 36 keys. 3×5 alphas plus 3 thumb keys per half.

Battery

You asked for a thin, watch-style rechargeable cell. Here are the options:

OptionThicknessCapacityVerdict
LIR2032 coin cell3.2 mm~40 mAhNot recommended. Its max charge current is about 20 mA, and the XIAO charges at 50 mA or more.
LIR2450 coin cell5.0 mm~120 mAhToo thick for the target.
Thin LiPo pouch (smartwatch style)2–3 mm100–300 mAhRecommended. For example 301230 (~100 mAh), 302030 (~150 mAh) or 402030 (~200 mAh, 4 mm).
  • The pouch code reads thickness / width / length, so 301230 is 3.0 × 12 × 30 mm.
  • Choose a cell with a protection PCB and bare leads or a JST-SH connector.
  • The XIAO charges at 50 mA by default (P0.13 left floating) or 100 mA (P0.13 driven LOW). Keep 50 mA for cells under 100 mAh.
  • Expected life with ZMK: the central half draws about 0.3–1 mA on average while in use. A 150 mAh cell lasts roughly 2–6 weeks on the central half and months on the other half. Sleep settings matter more than capacity.

Height budget

Bar lengths are to scale, using the middle of each range. The XIAO's height above the PCB must be measured. If it stands taller than switch plus keycap, drop it into a PCB cutout.

Open questions (resolve before routing)

  • Key pitch. Provisional 16.5 × 16.5 mm. The minimum is 14.7 mm, set by the land pattern. Confirm against the Kailh 1U keycap datasheet; chalk used about 16 × 16 mm caps.
  • Battery model. Choose a specific pouch cell and record its exact L × W × T.
  • XIAO mounting height. Surface-mount on the castellations or sit it in a PCB cutout. Measure the real part.
  • Keycaps. Stock Kailh PG1316S caps, or thinner custom 3D-printed caps.
  • Solder method. Pads only (hot plate and paste), or through-holes under the pads so you can solder from the back with an iron.
  • PCB strategy. One reversible PCB, or separate left and right PCBs.

01Layout & matrix

Each half is 5 columns × 4 rows on a uniform pitch. Keys only, that is 82.5 × 66 mm at 16.5 mm pitch. With about 1.5 mm of case wall on each side, a half comes to roughly 86 × 69 mm.

The XIAO slot is 33 × 16.5 mm and the XIAO is 21 × 17.8 mm, so it fits lengthwise with about 0.65 mm of overhang above and below. The USB-C port faces the outer side edge. Check this in the KiCad 3D viewer.

Matrix

  • A 5-column × 4-row diode matrix with 18 switches per half.
  • That uses 9 GPIO. The XIAO has 11 (D0–D10), which leaves 2 spare.
  • Diode direction is col2row, the ZMK default.
  • Use 1N4148W diodes (SOD-123) on the back. Use SOD-323 if they sit over the battery pocket.
  • Don't use the NFC pins (P0.09 / P0.10) unless NFC is disabled.

The right half mirrors the columns. Finalize the map while routing.

Other parts per half

  • Power switch. A small side-actuated slide switch (for example MSK-12C02) in series with BAT+, placed on the outer edge next to USB-C.
  • Reset button. Optional, since the XIAO has one. Add one if the XIAO will be enclosed.
  • Battery pads. BAT+ and BAT− run to the XIAO's back-side pads through the power switch.

02PCB design

Ergogen generates the key grid, the outlines and a placed but unrouted KiCad board from a YAML file. KiCad handles routing, design-rule checks and export. JLCPCB (or PCBWay) makes the boards.

Plated through-holes inside each switch pad, as on the Holykeebs practice board and in chalk, let you hand-solder every switch from underneath with an iron. Decide this before finalizing the footprint.

Steps

  1. Install the tools. KiCad 9 (8 also works), Node.js LTS and npm i -g ergogen (version 4.1+).
  2. Write ergogen/config.yaml. Set kx/ky to 16.5, add a 5×3 matrix zone and a 3-key thumb zone with no stagger or splay, add outlines for the board, case and battery pocket, and add PCB entries with template: kicad8. Then run ergogen ergogen/ -o output/.
  3. Set up the board in KiCad. 1.0 mm thick, 2 layers, 1 oz copper, with the JLCPCB design rules. Use 0.2 mm traces (0.25 mm for power) and 0.3 / 0.6 mm vias.
  4. Route. Columns on the front layer and rows on the back. Add a ground pour on both layers, and keep copper away from the XIAO antenna end. Add M2 holes or keep-outs for the case.
  5. Check. Run the design-rule check and look at the 3D viewer for collisions. Print the board 1:1 on paper and lay real parts on it.
  6. Order. Export the Gerbers and drill files. Order 1.0 mm FR4 with an ENIG finish (flatter pads help the switches sit level), quantity 5, plus a stencil if you'll use paste.

  • Keymap. Start from a Miryoku- or Callum-style layout with home-row mods and 3 thumbs per side, then adjust it in ZMK Studio without reflashing.
  • Flash. Double-tap reset on the XIAO to mount it as a drive, then copy that side's .uf2 onto it.
  • Test early. Before the case is done, short each row and column pair with tweezers to confirm every key.
  • If pairing sticks. Flash settings_reset to both halves, then reflash.

04Case

The case is parametric CAD written in code, using build123d (OpenSCAD is an alternative). It imports the Ergogen outline files so it always matches the PCB. Key pitch, PCB thickness, battery size, wall and floor thickness are all parameters.

Cross-section, not to scale. The wall rises only to the top of the PCB, so the switches and keycaps stand proud.
  • Tray. The floor and walls are one piece. The PCB drops in and rests on a ledge around the inside of the wall.
  • Retention. 3–4 M2 screws into heat-set inserts, or thin magnets, or a snap lip.
  • Support. Ribs or posts under the PCB between the diodes, so the 1.0 mm board doesn't flex when you type.
  • Cutouts. USB-C on the outer edge, the power switch slot and an optional reset hole.
  • Battery pocket in the floor. Hold the cell with Kapton tape or thin foam, and never compress it.
  • Feet. Rubber feet in recessed pockets, so they add no height.

05Assembly & test

Practise first.

The Holykeebs PG1316S practice board is cheap, and these switches are close to impossible to desolder.

  1. Inspect the bare PCB. Check for shorts between BAT+ and GND, and between VCC and GND.
  2. Diodes on the back. Check orientation before soldering, and use very little solder.
  3. XIAO. Solder the castellations flat. Flash the firmware now and confirm it shows up over USB.
  4. Test the matrix before the switches. Short each switch's pads with tweezers and confirm the key registers.
  5. Switches. Either paste and a hot plate at 260 °C or less for under 5 s, or solder from the back through the pad holes at 280 °C or less for 3 s or less. Use very little solder, since excess stops the switch sitting flat. Test each column as you finish it.
  6. Power switch and battery. Check polarity with a multimeter first. BAT− goes to the XIAO's BAT−, and BAT+ goes through the power switch to the XIAO's BAT+.
  7. Full test on battery. Pairing, every key, battery percentage, sleep and wake.
  8. Into the case. Fix the battery with Kapton or foam, then fit the PCB and screws.
  9. Keycaps. Optionally add a 0.5 mm Poron foam layer to quiet them (a tip from chalk).

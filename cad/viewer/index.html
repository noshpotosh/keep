What the model shows

  • A concept, not the locked design. It follows the Microsoft Designer Compact Keyboard: a slim slab with soft rounded edges, a top deck around every key, and caps standing 0.7 mm proud of the deck.
  • The deck needs a printable web between openings: 1.15–1.35 mm at 18 mm pitch. At the locked 17 mm pitch it would be about 0.15 mm, so this variant is 18 mm.
  • Two parts: a top shell (walls plus a 0.8 mm deck) on a flat bottom plate. The seam sits low on the side. The plate carries the ledge ring and posts the PCB rests on.
  • The face-down XIAO sits under plain deck in the empty thumb slot, with 0.8 mm to spare. Its reset pinhole stays in the plate.
  • The battery pocket, USB-C pocket and 7.0 mm height are the same as the open tray. USB-C is still low, with its centre about 1.9 mm above the desk.

What the model shows

  • An open tray with floating keys. The wall rises only to the PCB top, so there is no deck, and the caps are about 0.75 mm apart at 17 mm pitch.
  • The 0.8 mm PCB rests on a 1 mm perimeter ledge, with a support post under every key that isn't over the battery.
  • The battery is a 30 × 20 × 2.5 mm placeholder in a pocket through the floor, covered with Kapton. The diodes for the keys above it are moved to the pocket's edge.
  • The XIAO is face-down on the PCB, with its shield and USB-C hanging through a cutout into a thinned floor pocket. A pinhole in the floor reaches its reset button (approximate position).
  • The USB-C sits low, with its centre about 1.9 mm above the desk. A standard plug's overmold is taller than that, so check cable clearance.
  • Switches are simple blocks at datasheet size, not the full Kailh mechanism.
